data:
  word: Compunction
  definitions:
  - part_of_speech: Noun
    definitions:
    - A feeling of guilt or moral scruple that prevents or follows the doing of something
      bad.
    synonyms:
    - Remorse
    - Regret
    - Guilt
    antonyms:
    - Indifference
    - Apathy
    - Unremorseful
    examples:
    - He felt compunction after lying to his friend.
    - She acted without compunction, betraying her colleagues.
    mnemonics:
    - '"Compunction" rhymes with "unction," which is associated with religious or
      moral behavior, emphasizing its connection to guilt or moral unease.'
    - Think of "con" in "compunction" as a reminder of the deceit or wrongdoing that
      often leads to guilty feelings.
  etymology:
    origin: Middle English from Latin 'compunctio', from the verb 'compungere' meaning
      'to prick severely', from 'com-' (expressing intensive force) + 'pungere' meaning
      'to prick'.
    historical_usage: The word 'compunction' originated in Old English and has retained
      its essential meaning over the centuries, referring to a feeling of guilt or
      moral unease.
  idioms:
  - expression: '"Without compunction"'
    explanation: Describing someone's behavior characterized by a lack of guilt or
      moral scruples.
  regional_variations: []
  misconceptions:
  - misconception: Confusing 'compunction' with 'complications' or 'conjunction.'
    explanation: '''Compunction'' refers to guilt or moral unease, whereas ''complications''
      refers to difficulties or complex situations, and ''conjunction'' refers to
      the joining or combining of things.'
