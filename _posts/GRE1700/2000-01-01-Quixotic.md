categories: [GRE, GRE_SET_2, TOEFL, English]
data:
  word: Quixotic
  definitions:
  - part_of_speech: Adjective
    definitions:
    - Extremely idealistic; unrealistic and impractical.
    synonyms:
    - Idealistic
    - Visionary
    - Romantic
    - Impractical
    antonyms:
    - Practical
    - Realistic
    - Pragmatic
    examples:
    - Sarah's quixotic dreams of changing the world often clashed with the harsh realities
      of life.
    - The quixotic adventure of pursuing a career as an artist without any financial
      stability worried her parents.
    mnemonics:
    - Think of the character Don Quixote who had unrealistic and extravagant ideals,
      which led him to engage in impractical and fantastical adventures.
  etymology:
    origin: The term "quixotic" is derived from the name of the main character in
      Miguel de Cervantes' novel "Don Quixote" (1605). The protagonist, Don Quixote,
      is a typically deluded idealist who embarks on ridiculous chivalric quests and
      fails to see reality. The adjective "quixotic" first appeared in the late 18th
      century in English to describe such behavior.
    historical_usage: Since its inception, "quixotic" has been used to describe people,
      ideas, or actions that romanticize or pursue lofty goals without practicality
      or awareness of reality.
  idioms:
  - expression: '"A quixotic endeavor"'
    explanation: Referring to a task or pursuit that is unrealistic, impractical,
      or unlikely to succeed. It alludes to the adventures undertaken by Don Quixote.
  regional_variations: []
  misconceptions:
  - misconception: Confusing "quixotic" with "quizzical."
    explanation: Although both words sound similar, "quixotic" relates to idealism
      and impracticality, while "quizzical" denotes an expression or manner indicating
      mild or amused puzzlement.
