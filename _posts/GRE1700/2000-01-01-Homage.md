  etymology:
    origin: Middle English from Old French, from home, homme 'man' (used as a noun
      in some medieval Latin phrases) + -age (denoting action or collectively the
      result of an action).
    historical_usage: The word originated from the Latin phrase "homagium," which
      referred to the medieval oath of fealty and homage rendered by a vassal to their
      lord.
  idioms:
  - expression: '"Pay homage"'
    explanation: To show respect or give honor to someone or something.
  regional_variations: []
  misconceptions:
  - misconception: Confusing "homage" with "homogenize."
    explanation: While the words may sound similar, they have different meanings.
      "Homage" refers to showing respect or honoring, while "homogenize" means to
      make uniform or blend together.
