data:
  word: Rigor
  definitions:
  - part_of_speech: Noun
    definitions:
    - The quality of being extremely thorough, accurate, or precise.
    - Rigidity or stiffness of a body or material.
    synonyms:
    - Exactness
    - Precision
    - Meticulousness
    antonyms:
    - Leniency
    - Laxity
    - Flexibility
    examples:
    - The scientist approached the experiment with rigor, ensuring every detail was
      accounted for.
    - The rigor of the steel beam made it resistant to bending under pressure.
    mnemonics:
    - Think of rigor as being strict and precise.
    - Rigor mortis, a term used to describe the stiffening of muscles after death,
      can remind you of the meaning of rigor.
  etymology:
    origin: 'Middle English (in the sense ''stiffness, rigidity''): from Latin rigor,
      from rigere ''be stiff or rigid''.'
    historical_usage: The word has roots in Latin and has been used in English since
      the 14th century, initially referring to physical stiffness. Over time, it gained
      a figurative sense to describe strictness and meticulousness.
  idioms:
  - expression: '"Rigor mortis"'
    explanation: A Latin term used to describe the stiffening of muscles after death,
      reflecting the original meaning of rigor.
  regional_variations: []
  misconceptions:
  - misconception: Confusing rigor with rigor mortis.
    explanation: While rigor mortis is a specific term for the postmortem stiffening
      of muscles, rigor has a broader meaning related to strictness, thoroughness,
      and precision in various contexts. It is not limited to the context of death.
