    origin: Middle English, from Old French 'repugnance', from Latin 'repugnantia',

    historical_usage: The word has been in use since the 15th century and has maintained
      its meaning throughout history.
  idioms:
  - expression: '"Feel repugnance"'
    explanation: To experience strong feelings of disgust or aversion.
  regional_variations: []
  misconceptions:
  - misconception: Interchanging "repugnance" with "repulsion."
    explanation: While related, repugnance refers to a strong feeling of disgust or
      aversion, whereas repulsion emphasizes the act of pushing away or recoiling
      from something.
