  word: Assuage

  - part_of_speech: Verb

    - Alleviate
    - Ease
    - Relieve
    antonyms:
    - Intensify
    - Aggravate
    - Exacerbate

    origin: Middle English from Old French "assaier" (to test), from Vulgar Latin
      "exagiare" (to weigh), influenced by Latin "ad-" (to) and "suavis" (sweet).
    historical_usage: The word "assuage" first appeared in English in the 14th century,
      with its meaning aligning closely to its current usage.
  idioms:
  - expression: '"Assuage one''s thirst"'
    explanation: To quench or satisfy one's thirst.
  regional_variations: []
  misconceptions:
  - misconception: Confusing "assuage" with "exacerbate."
    explanation: While "assuage" means to make something less intense or severe, "exacerbate"
