data:
  word: Maladroit
  definitions:
  - part_of_speech: Adjective
    definitions:
    - Lacking in skill or dexterity; clumsy.
    synonyms:
    - Clumsy
    - Inept
    - Awkward
    - Unskillful
    antonyms:
    - Dexterous
    - Skillful
    - Adept
    examples:
    - He was so maladroit with his hands that he often dropped things.
    - Despite her best efforts, she remained maladroit in playing the piano.
    mnemonics:
    - Picture someone with two left hands trying to juggle, representing a lack of
      skill or dexterity.
  etymology:
    origin: The word "maladroit" comes from the French words "mal" meaning "bad" and
      "adroit" meaning "skillful". It entered the English language in the early 18th
      century.
    historical_usage: The term has consistently been used to describe a lack of skill
      or clumsiness throughout its history.
  idioms:
  - expression: '"Maladroitly handling something"'
    explanation: Refers to clumsily or ineptly handling or dealing with something.
  regional_variations: []
  misconceptions:
  - misconception: Confusing "maladroit" with "maladroitness"
    explanation: '"Maladroit" is the adjective form that describes a person or their
      actions as lacking skill. "Maladroitness" is the noun form referring to the
      state of being awkward or clumsy.'
