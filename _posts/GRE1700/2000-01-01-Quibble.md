  word: Quibble

  - part_of_speech: Noun
    definitions:

    - A playfully or deliberately evasive or frivolous argument.
    synonyms:
    - Nitpick
    - Cavil
    - Pettifogging
    antonyms:
    - Acceptance
    - Agreement
    - Satisfaction
    examples:
    - He raised a quibble about the wording of the contract.
    - Their argument was full of quibbles and little substance.

    - Think of someone "quibbling" over small details.
    - Picture a person actively dodging the main point and engaging in trivial arguments.
  etymology:
    origin: The word "quibble" originated in the late 16th century from the Middle
      English word "quiblen", which meant to play on words or equivocate. It is derived
      from the Middle Low German word "quibbele", meaning a playful verbal trick or
      dispute.
    historical_usage: The word has been used consistently over the centuries to describe
      objections, frivolous arguments, or wordplay.
  idioms:
  - expression: '"Quibbling over straws"'
    explanation: Engaging in unnecessary, trivial arguments or objections over minor
      details.
  regional_variations: []
  misconceptions:
  - misconception: Considering a quibble to be a significant objection or valid criticism.
    explanation: A quibble refers to a trivial matter or a superficial argument, not
