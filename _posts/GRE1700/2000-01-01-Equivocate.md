data:
  word: Equivocate
  definitions:
  - part_of_speech: Verb
    definitions:
    - To use ambiguous language so as to conceal the truth or avoid committing oneself;
      to be deliberately ambiguous or unclear.
    synonyms:
    - Hedge
    - Prevaricate
    - Dodge
    - Evade
    antonyms:
    - Clarify
    - Explain
    - Be straightforward
    examples:
    - The politician equivocated when asked about his stance on the controversial
      issue.
    - She always equivocates to avoid taking a definitive position in arguments.
    mnemonics:
    - Think of "equi" as equal, suggesting a balance between two opposing arguments.
    - '"Equivocate" sounds similar to "evasive," which reflects the act of avoiding
      commitment or clarity.'
  etymology:
    origin: The word "equivocate" comes from the Latin word "aequivocare," which is
      a combination of "aevus" (equal) and "vox" (voice). It originally meant "to
      use words of double meaning." In English, it appeared in the late 16th century.
    historical_usage: The term has been used to describe the act of speaking ambiguously
      or evasively since its introduction into the English language.
  idioms:
  - expression: '"Equivocate on something"'
    explanation: To intentionally use ambiguous language specifically about a certain
      issue or topic.
  regional_variations: []
  misconceptions:
  - misconception: Equating equivocation with dishonesty or lying.
    explanation: While equivocation can involve an intention to deceive, it can also
      stem from a desire to avoid conflict or commitment. Equivocating does not always
      imply outright lying, but rather speaking in a way that is deliberately unclear
      or ambiguous.
