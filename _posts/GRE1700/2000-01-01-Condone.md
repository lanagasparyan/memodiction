data:
  word: Condone
  definitions:
  - part_of_speech: Verb
    definitions:
    - To accept and allow (behavior that is considered morally wrong or offensive)
      to continue without punishment or disapproval.
    - To approve or sanction, especially with reluctance.
    synonyms:
    - Tolerate
    - Overlook
    - Excuse
    antonyms:
    - Condemn
    - Disapprove
    - Punish
    examples:
    - Despite his inappropriate comments, his behavior was condoned by the company.
    - The coach condoned the use of performance-enhancing drugs among the athletes.
    mnemonics:
    - '"Condone" sounds like "cone," imagine a traffic cone allowing wrong behavior
      to pass.'
    - Think of "condone" as "con-dome" where you accept something despite knowing
      it is not ideal or morally acceptable.
  etymology:
    origin: The word "condone" originated from the Latin word "condonare," meaning
      "to give up, to remit." It came into use in English during the early 19th century.
    historical_usage: The term has retained its general meaning over time, indicating
      the acceptance or approval of something, often with reluctance or reservations.
  idioms:
  - expression: '"Turn a blind eye to"'
    explanation: Similar to condoning, this phrase means to deliberately ignore or
      disregard something, usually wrongdoing or misconduct.
  regional_variations: []
  misconceptions:
  - misconception: Some people mistakenly believe that "condone" implies full support
