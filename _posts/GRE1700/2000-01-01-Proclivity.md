categories: [GRE, GRE_SET_2, TOEFL, English]
data:
  word: Proclivity
  definitions:
  - part_of_speech: Noun
    definitions:
    - A tendency to choose or do something regularly; an inclination or predisposition
      towards a particular thing.
    synonyms:
    - Inclination
    - Tendency
    - Predisposition
    antonyms:
    - Aversion
    - Dislike
    - Disinclination
    examples:
    - She has a proclivity for adventure and is always seeking out new experiences.
    - His proclivity for procrastination often leads to missed deadlines.
    mnemonics:
    - Proclivity starts with "pro," which can be associated with being in favor of
      something or having a positive inclination towards it.
  etymology:
    origin: The word "proclivity" comes from the Latin word "proclivitas," which means
      a natural tendency or inclination. It is derived from the combination of "pro"
      (forward) and "clivus" (slope or incline).
    historical_usage: The term has been in use since the 1620s and has retained its
      meaning throughout the centuries.
  idioms:
  - expression: '"Inclined by proclivity"'
    explanation: Indicates a tendency or inclination driven by one's inherent predisposition
      towards something.
  regional_variations: []
  misconceptions:
  - misconception: Using "proclivity" interchangeably with "preference" or "desire."
    explanation: While similar, "proclivity" refers to a natural inclination, whereas
      "preference" and "desire" are more subjective choices based on personal tastes
      or conscious decision-making.
