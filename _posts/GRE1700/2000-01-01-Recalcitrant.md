    - Defiant

    - Compliant

  etymology:
    origin: The word "recalcitrant" originated in the early 19th century from the
      Latin word "recalcitrare," which means 'to kick out.' It is derived from the
      combination of the prefix "re-" (back, against) and "calcitrare" (kick).
    historical_usage: The term has been used consistently since its introduction in
      the early 1800s to describe individuals or entities that resist authority or
      discipline.
  idioms:
  - expression: '"Recalcitrant behavior"'
    explanation: Refers to the act of displaying uncooperative and defiant actions
      toward authorities or established norms.
  regional_variations: []
  misconceptions:
  - misconception: Confusing "recalcitrant" with "obstinate" or "stubborn."
    explanation: While "recalcitrant" shares similar characteristics with obstinacy
      and stubbornness, it specifically implies a strong resistance toward authority
      or discipline. It often refers to individuals who actively challenge and oppose
      established rules or regulations.
