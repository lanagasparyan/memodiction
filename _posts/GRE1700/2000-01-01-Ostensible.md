categories: [GRE, GRE_SET_2, TOEFL, English]
data:
  word: Ostensible
  definitions:
  - part_of_speech: Adjective
    definitions:
    - Stated or appearing to be true, but not necessarily so.
    synonyms:
    - Apparent
    - Seeming
    - Alleged
    antonyms:
    - Actual
    - Real
    - Genuine
    examples:
    - The ostensible reason for his absence was illness, but many suspected it was
      a cover-up.
    - The ostensible purpose of the meeting was to discuss the new project, but it
      turned out to be a surprise party.
    mnemonics:
    - '"Osten-" sounds similar to "aston-", so think of something that seems astonishing
      but may not be true.'
    - Picture a person wearing a fake mask that looks real, representing something
      ostensible.
  etymology:
    origin: Late Middle English from Old French 'ostensible', from Latin 'ostensibilis',
      from 'ostendere' meaning "to show".
    historical_usage: The word has been used in English since the late 15th century,
      primarily retaining its meaning of something that is shown or presented as true,
      though not necessarily being so.
  idioms:
  - expression: '"Ostensible reason"'
    explanation: Refers to the apparent or stated reason for something, which may
      not be the true or underlying reason.
  regional_variations: []
  misconceptions:
  - misconception: Confusing ostensible with genuine or real.
    explanation: While something ostensible may appear to be true, it is not necessarily
      genuine or real.
