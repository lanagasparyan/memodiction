  word: Prolix

  - part_of_speech: Adjective
    definitions:
    - (of speech or writing) using or containing too many words; tediously lengthy.
    synonyms:
    - Wordy
    - Verbose
    - Long-winded
    antonyms:
    - Concise
    - Succinct
    examples:
    - His prolix explanations made it difficult to understand the main point.
    - The author's prolix writing style made the book tedious to read.
    mnemonics:
    - '"Prolix" sounds like "prolonged," which can remind you of long and wordy explanations.'
    - Think of "Prolix" as a verbose speech full of unnecessary words.
  etymology:
    origin: The word "prolix" comes from the Latin word "prolixus," meaning "extended"
      or "extended backward." It entered the English language in the late 16th century.
    historical_usage: '"Prolix" has been used to describe long-winded speeches and
      writings throughout history, emphasizing the negative aspect of excessive words.'
  idioms:
  - expression: '"Prolixity of speech"'
    explanation: This phrase refers to the tendency to use too many words when speaking,
      often resulting in a lack of conciseness and clarity.
  regional_variations: []
  misconceptions:
  - misconception: Confusing "prolix" with "complex."
    explanation: While both words can indicate an abundance of words or information,
      "prolix" specifically refers to unnecessary lengthiness or tediousness, whereas
      "complex" refers to something intricate or difficult to understand.
