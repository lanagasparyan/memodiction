categories: [GRE, GRE_SET_2, TOEFL, English]
data:
  word: Demagogue
  definitions:
  - part_of_speech: Noun
    definitions:
    - A political leader who appeals to people's emotions, prejudices, and fears rather
      than their rationality and intelligence.
    synonyms:
    - Agitator
    - Rabble-rouser
    - Populist
    antonyms:
    - Statesman
    - Diplomat
    - Moderate
    examples:
    - The demagogue used fear tactics to manipulate the public and gain support for
      his extreme policies.
    - Many people were captivated by the demagogue's charismatic speeches, despite
      the lack of substance in his arguments.
    mnemonics:
    - Think of a demagogue as someone who manipulates the emotions of the crowd.
    - Break down the word "demagogue" into "demo" (people) and "agogue" (leading),
      reminding you that a demagogue leads the people by playing on their emotions.
  etymology:
    origin: The word "demagogue" originated from the Greek word "dēmagōgós," which
      means "leader of the people." The term was initially neutral, referring to a
      leader who influenced and guided the people.
    historical_usage: Over time, the term gained a negative connotation due to examples
      of leaders who exploited the emotions and prejudices of the people for their
      own benefit, often disregarding rational discourse. The term has been used extensively
      in political discourse and analysis.
  idioms:
  - expression: '"Playing to the gallery"'
    explanation: This phrase refers to a demagogue's manipulation of public opinion
      by appealing to popular sentiment or prejudice rather than addressing substantive
      issues.
  regional_variations: []
  misconceptions:
  - misconception: Some people confuse demagogues with effective, charismatic leaders.
    explanation: While charismatic leaders can inspire and motivate, demagogues specifically
      rely on emotional appeals and often manipulate their followers by playing on
      fears and prejudices rather than logical arguments or evidence-based policies.
      Demagogues may or may not possess true leadership qualities or a genuine concern
      for the well-being of their constituents.
