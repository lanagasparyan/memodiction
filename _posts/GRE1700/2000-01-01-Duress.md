---
categories: [GRE, GRE_SET_2, TOEFL, English]
data:
  word: Duress
  definitions:
  - part_of_speech: Noun
    definitions:
    - Threats, violence, constraints, or other action used to coerce someone into
      doing something against their will or better judgment.
    synonyms:
    - Coercion
    - Compulsion
    - Force
    - Pressure
    - Intimidation
    antonyms:
    - Free will
    - Consent
    - Agreement
    examples:
    - The suspect claimed that he confessed under duress.
    - The victim signed the contract under duress.
    mnemonics:
    - '"Duress" sounds like "duress" which represents the feeling of being threatened
      or forced.'
  etymology:
    origin: Middle English, derived from Old French "duresse" and Latin "duritia"
      meaning "hardness", later from Latin "durus" meaning "hard".
    historical_usage: The term originated in the Middle Ages and has been used consistently
      since then to refer to situations where individuals are under pressure to act
      against their will.
  idioms:
  - expression: '"Under duress"'
    explanation: This phrase indicates that someone is being forced or coerced to
      do something against their will.
  regional_variations: []
  misconceptions:
  - misconception: Confusing "duress" with "stress".
    explanation: While "duress" often involves stress or pressure, it specifically
      refers to threats, violence, or coercion used to compel someone's actions. Stress,
      on the other hand, can be unrelated to external factors and can have different
      causes.
