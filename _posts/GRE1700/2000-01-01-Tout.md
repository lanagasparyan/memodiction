data:
  word: Tout
  definitions:
  - part_of_speech: Verb
    definitions:
    - To attempt to sell or advertise (something), typically by pestering people in
      an aggressive or bold manner.
    synonyms:
    - Promote
    - Endorse
    - Pitch
    antonyms:
    - Discourage
    - Dissuade
    - Deter
    examples:
    - The street vendor was touting his handmade jewelry to passersby.
    - The politician was touting their new policy during the campaign rally.
    mnemonics:
    - Think of someone loudly promoting a product, as if they're "shouting" about
      it.
  etymology:
    origin: The word "tout" originated from the Middle English word "tuten," which
      meant "to peep" or "to look out."
    historical_usage: The word has been used since the 15th century, originally referring
      to someone who would watch for favored horses in a race and solicit bets. Over
      time, it evolved to encompass aggressively promoting or advertising anything,
      not just horse racing-related activities.
  idioms:
  - expression: '"Tout someone''s praises"'
    explanation: To speak highly or boastfully about someone or their abilities.
  regional_variations: []
  misconceptions:
  - misconception: Confusing "tout" with "taut."
    explanation: '"Tout" refers to aggressively promoting or selling something, while
      "taut" means tightly stretched or tense. They have different spellings and meanings.'
