    origin: The word "meddlesome" comes from the combination of the words "meddle"
      and "some." "Meddle" originated from Middle English "medelen," which is derived
      from Old English "meddlian," meaning "to have to do with" or "to mix oneself
      in." The suffix "-some" is added to convey the meaning of "tending to" or "characterized
      by."
    historical_usage: The word "meddlesome" has been in use since the 16th century,
      indicating a long history of describing those who intrusively involve themselves
      in others' affairs.
  idioms:
  - expression: '"Meddlesome parker"'
    explanation: This phrase refers to someone who unnecessarily interferes with or

  misconceptions:

    explanation: The term "meddlesome" inherently implies interference that is unwelcome
      or intrusive, even if the person's intentions may be well-meaning. It should
      not be confused with helpfulness or genuine concern.
