categories: [GRE, GRE_SET_2, TOEFL, English]
data:
  word: Repudiate
  definitions:
  - part_of_speech: Verb
    definitions:
    - To reject or disown something completely; to refuse to accept or acknowledge
      something; to deny the validity or truthfulness of something.
    synonyms:
    - Reject
    - Disown
    - Renounce
    - Deny
    - Discard
    - Decline
    antonyms:
    - Accept
    - Acknowledge
    - Embrace
    - Validate
    - Affirm
    - Approve
    examples:
    - The politician repudiated the claims made against him.
    - She decided to repudiate her former beliefs and adopt a new ideology.
    mnemonics:
    - Remember that 'repudiate' sounds similar to "re-poo-dee-ate," implying the act
      of forcefully rejecting something.
    - Think of the word 'repudiate' as "re-pu-di-ate," where 'di' represents the denial
      or disowning of something.
  etymology:
    origin: Late Middle English via Latin from repudiare, from repudium 'divorce,
      rejection', from re- 'back' + pudere 'be ashamed'.
    historical_usage: The term 'repudiate' originated in the late Middle English period
      and can be traced back to Latin roots. It has been used consistently throughout
      history with its original meaning intact.
  idioms:
  - expression: '"Repudiate a claim"'
    explanation: To formally reject or deny the validity of a claim, usually in a
      legal or official context.
  regional_variations: []
  misconceptions:
  - misconception: Confusing 'repudiate' with 'refute'.
    explanation: While 'repudiate' refers to completely rejecting or disowning something,
      'refute' means to prove something wrong or false through evidence or argument.
      It is important to differentiate between these two terms to avoid confusion.
