data:
  word: Demur
  definitions:
  - part_of_speech: Verb
    definitions:
    - To raise objections or show reluctance.
    synonyms:
    - Object
    - Protest
    - Hesitate
    antonyms:
    - Agree
    - Comply
    - Consent
    examples:
    - John demurred when asked to testify in court.
    - She demurred at the idea of going on a roller coaster.
    mnemonics:
    - '"De" in demur can be a reminder of "defying" or "disagreeing" with something.'
    - Think of "demur" as saying "I'm not so sure about that" or "I have reservations".
  etymology:
    origin: Middle English, from Old French 'demurer', from Latin 'demorari' (to delay),
      from 'de-' (from) + 'morari' (to delay)
    historical_usage: The word originated from Latin, and over time, its usage evolved
      to its current meaning of raising objections or showing reluctance.
  idioms:
  - expression: '"Without demur"'
    explanation: This phrase means without hesitation or objection. For example, "He
      followed the instructions without demur."
  regional_variations: []
  misconceptions:
  - misconception: Confusing "demur" with "demure".
    explanation: While they sound similar, "demur" and "demure" have different meanings.
      "Demur" refers to raising objections or showing reluctance, while "demure" means
      being reserved, modest, or shy.
