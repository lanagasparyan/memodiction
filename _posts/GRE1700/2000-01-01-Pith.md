categories: [GRE, GRE_SET_2, TOEFL, English]
data:
  word: Pith
  definitions:
  - part_of_speech: Noun
    definitions:
    - The essential or central part of something; the most important or relevant aspect
      of a topic or issue.
    synonyms:
    - Essence
    - Core
    - Heart
    antonyms:
    - Frill
    - Superficiality
    examples:
    - The pith of the argument was that we needed to prioritize education.
    - The pith of the article was lost in the unnecessary details.
    mnemonics:
    - '"Pith" sounds like "the pith" which represents the essential part.'
  etymology:
    origin: Old English 'piþa', meaning "the spongy substance in plants," from Proto-Germanic
      'piþan' (source also of Old High German 'pfitan', Middle Dutch 'pitte', Dutch
      'pit', German 'Pitze'), from PIE root '*peie-' meaning "to be fat, swell".
    historical_usage: The word "pith" originated in Old English and has retained its
      meaning as the central part of something.
  idioms:
  - expression: '"To get to the pith of the matter"'
    explanation: This phrase means to understand the central or most important aspect
      of a situation or problem.
  regional_variations: []
  misconceptions:
  - misconception: Confusing "pith" with "pitch".
    explanation: '"Pith" refers to the essential or core part of something, while
      "pitch" typically refers to the act of throwing or selling something. These
      words are unrelated and have different meanings.'
