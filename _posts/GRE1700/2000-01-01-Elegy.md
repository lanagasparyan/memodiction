  - part_of_speech: Noun

    - A mournful or melancholic musical composition.
    synonyms:
    - Dirge
    - Requiem
    - Lament
    - Threnody
    antonyms:
    - Celebration
    - Ode

    - He wrote an elegy in memory of his late grandmother.
    - The composer created a beautiful elegy for the fallen soldiers.
    mnemonics:
    - Think of "elegy" as a sorrowful poem or song for the deceased, which evokes
      emotions of grief.
    - Remember the word "eulogy" which is also related to death and funeral, but has
      a positive tone, unlike "elegy."
  etymology:
    origin: The word "elegy" originated from the Greek word "elegeia," meaning a lament
      or mournful song.
    historical_usage: Elegies have a long history, dating back to ancient Greece and
      Rome, where they were often sung at funerals. They became popular forms of mourning
      and memorialization in literature during the Renaissance and have continued
      to be used in modern times.
  idioms:
  - expression: '"Elegy written in a country churchyard"'
    explanation: Refers to a famous poem by Thomas Gray, which reflects on the lives
      and deaths of ordinary people buried in a rural churchyard.
  regional_variations: []
  misconceptions:
  - misconception: Confusing an elegy with a eulogy.
    explanation: While both words deal with themes of death and remembrance, a eulogy
      is a speech or piece of writing that praises or celebrates the life of the deceased,
      whereas an elegy expresses sorrow and mourning.
