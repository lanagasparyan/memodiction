categories: [GRE, GRE_SET_2, TOEFL, English]
data:
  word: Disingenuous
  definitions:
  - part_of_speech: Adjective
    definitions:
    - Not candid or sincere, typically by pretending that one knows less about something
      than one really does.
    synonyms:
    - Deceptive
    - Insincere
    - 'False'
    - Dishonest
    antonyms:
    - Genuine
    - Honest
    - Sincere
    - Candid
    examples:
    - His compliments were so exaggerated that they seemed disingenuous.
    - She gave a disingenuous apology, pretending she didn't know the impact of her
      actions.
    mnemonics:
    - '"Dis" means not or opposite, so disingenuous means not genuine or sincere.'
    - Think of someone putting on a fake smile to mask their true feelings.
  etymology:
    origin: The word "disingenuous" originated in the early 17th century from Latin
      "disingenuus," which means 'not of noble birth.'
    historical_usage: The word has been used to describe someone who is deceitful
      or misleading in their behavior or speech.
  idioms:
  - expression: '"Disingenuous smile"'
    explanation: Referring to a smile that does not genuinely reflect the person's
      true feelings or intentions.
  regional_variations: []
  misconceptions:
  - misconception: Confusing "disingenuous" with "ingenuous"
    explanation: While "disingenuous" means not genuine or sincere, "ingenuous" means
      innocent, naïve, or artless. They have opposite meanings, even though they sound
      similar.
