---
data:
  word: Simile
  definitions:
  - part_of_speech: Noun
    definitions:
    - A figure of speech involving the comparison of one thing with another thing
      of a different kind, used to make a description more emphatic or vivid.
    synonyms:
    - Analogy
    - Metaphor
    - Figure of speech
    antonyms:
    - Literal
    examples:
    - Her smile was as bright as the sun.
    - The waves crashed against the shore like thunder.
    mnemonics:
    - Simile starts with "s" and so does "comparison."
    - Think of "similar" to remember simile.
  etymology:
    origin: The word "simile" comes from the Latin word "similis," meaning "like"
      or "similar."
    historical_usage: Similes have been used in various literary works and are a common
      rhetorical device in both ancient and modern writing.
  idioms:
  - expression: '"Like a needle in a haystack"'
    explanation: Refers to something that is extremely difficult or impossible to
      find.
  regional_variations: []
  misconceptions:
  - misconception: Confusing simile with metaphor.
    explanation: While both simile and metaphor involve making comparisons, simile
      uses "like" or "as" to make the comparison explicit, whereas metaphor directly
      states that one thing is another.
