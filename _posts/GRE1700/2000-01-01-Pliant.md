    origin: Middle English "plyant," from Old French "pliant," from Latin "plicāre"

    historical_usage: The word "pliant" has been used since the 14th century to describe
      something that can be easily bent or shaped. It later gained the figurative
      sense of being easily influenced or yielding.
  idioms:
  - expression: '"Bend over backward"'
    explanation: This phrase means to make a great effort, often to accommodate or
      please someone. It can be used to describe a pliant or flexible attitude.
  regional_variations: []
  misconceptions:
  - misconception: Confusing "pliant" with "compliant."
    explanation: While both words are related to being easily influenced or yielding,
      "pliant" primarily describes physical flexibility, whereas "compliant" specifically
      refers to someone's willingness to obey rules or requests.
