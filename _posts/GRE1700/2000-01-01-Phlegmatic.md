  etymology:
    origin: The word "phlegmatic" comes from the ancient Greek word "phlegmatikos"
      which means "abounding in phlegm." The concept of humorism in ancient Greek
      medicine associated phlegm with calmness and apathy. This usage of "phlegmatic"
      to describe a temperament or personality trait dates back to the 14th century.
    historical_usage: In ancient times, phlegmatic temperament was one of the four
      humors believed to influence personality and health. It was believed that an
      excess of phlegm in the body would result in a calm and unemotional temperament.
  idioms:
  - expression: '"Keep a phlegmatic attitude"'
    explanation: This phrase suggests maintaining a calm and composed demeanor in

  misconceptions:

      to a specific temperament characterized by calmness and emotional stability.
