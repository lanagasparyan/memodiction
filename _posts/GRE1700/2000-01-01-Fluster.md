data:
  word: Fluster
  definitions:
  - part_of_speech: Verb
    definitions:
    - To make (someone) agitated or confused.
    synonyms:
    - Rattle
    - Unsettle
    - Disconcert
    antonyms:
    - Calm
    - Compose
    - Reassure
    examples:
    - The unexpected question flustered the interviewee.
    - Trying to juggle too many tasks at once can easily fluster anyone.
    mnemonics:
    - '"Fluster" sounds like "flutter," which symbolizes agitation or confusion.'
    - Imagine a person being surrounded by a flurry of papers and feeling flustered.
  etymology:
    origin: It originated from the English dialect term "flustre" or "flusteren,"
      which meant to fluster or confuse.
    historical_usage: The word has been used since at least the early 17th century,
      primarily in British and American English.
  idioms:
  - expression: '"All in a fluster"'
    explanation: To be in a state of agitation or confusion.
  regional_variations: []
  misconceptions:
  - misconception: Confusing "fluster" with "flutter."
    explanation: While "fluster" and "flutter" sound similar and may denote similar
      states of agitation, they have distinct meanings. "Fluster" implies a mix of
      agitation and confusion, while "flutter" refers to a lighter, more rapid movement
      or excitement.
