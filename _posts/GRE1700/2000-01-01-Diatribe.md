data:
  word: Diatribe
  definitions:
  - part_of_speech: Noun
    definitions:
    - A bitter and abusive speech or piece of writing; a harsh and critical attack.
    synonyms:
    - Tirade
    - Rant
    - Harangue
    antonyms:
    - Praise
    - Compliment
    examples:
    - The politician delivered a scathing diatribe against his opponent during the
      debate.
    - The newspaper article was filled with diatribes against the government's policies.
    mnemonics:
    - '"Diatribe" sounds like "die tribe," which can be associated with a hostile
      verbal attack targeting a particular group or individual.'
    - Think of the "D" in "diatribe" as representing "disparaging" or "disapproving"
      speech.
  etymology:
    origin: The word "diatribe" originated from the Greek word "diatribē," meaning
      a prolonged discourse or debate. It evolved in Latin to mean a learned discussion
      and eventually gained its current negative connotation.
    historical_usage: The word "diatribe" has been used since the mid-16th century
      to describe a bitter and abusive speech or writing.
  idioms:
  - expression: '"Launch into a diatribe"'
    explanation: To begin or commence a harsh and critical verbal attack or rant.
  regional_variations: []
  misconceptions:
  - misconception: Using "diatribe" interchangeably with a simple criticism or disagreement.
    explanation: A diatribe goes beyond constructive criticism or healthy disagreement.
      It refers to a harsh, abusive, and often lengthy attack or rant.
