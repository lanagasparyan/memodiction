  - part_of_speech: Verb

  etymology:
    origin: Subsume comes from the Latin word "subsumere," which combines "sub" (under)
      and "sumere" (take), meaning "to take under." It entered the English language
      in the mid-18th century.
    historical_usage: The term subsume has consistently carried the meaning of including
      or absorbing something within something else throughout its history.
  idioms:
  - expression: '"Subsume under"'
    explanation: Refers to including or incorporating one concept, idea, or entity
      within another, often used in academic or philosophical discussions.
  regional_variations: []
  misconceptions:
  - misconception: Confusing "subsume" with "assume."
    explanation: While both words have a slight phonetic similarity, "subsume" and
      "assume" have different meanings and should not be used interchangeably. "Subsume"
      refers to including or absorbing something within something else, while "assume"
      means to take on or adopt a belief or role without proof or evidence.
