  word: Obdurate

  - part_of_speech: Adjective

    - Inflexible
    - Unyielding
    - Obstinate
    antonyms:
    - Flexible

    - Think of the word "obdurate" as an obstinate and hard shell that refuses to

  etymology:
    origin: Middle English, from Latin "obduratus", past participle of "obdurare"
      (to harden)
    historical_usage: The word "obdurate" has been in usage since the 14th century.
      It comes from the Latin word "obduratus" which means "hardened" or "firm". Over
      time, it has retained its meaning of being unyielding or stubborn.
  idioms:
  - expression: '"Obdurate heart"'
    explanation: Referring to someone whose heart is hardened and unwilling to show
      compassion or empathy.
  regional_variations: []
  misconceptions:
  - misconception: Confusing "obdurate" with "obtuse".
    explanation: While both words refer to a lack of willingness to change, "obdurate"
