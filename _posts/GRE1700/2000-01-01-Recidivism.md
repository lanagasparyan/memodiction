data:
  word: Recidivism
  definitions:
  - part_of_speech: Noun
    definitions:
    - The tendency of a convicted criminal to reoffend.
    synonyms:
    - Relapse
    - Backsliding
    - Repeat offending
    antonyms:
    - Rehabilitation
    - Reintegration
    - Redemption
    examples:
    - The high recidivism rate among ex-convicts is a significant challenge for our
      criminal justice system.
    - The prison's rehabilitation programs aim to reduce recidivism by providing inmates
      with education and job training.
    mnemonics:
    - Recidivism sounds like "re-" (again) + "cid" (cidere in Latin means "to fall").
      So, it refers to the act of falling back into criminal behavior.
  etymology:
    origin: The word "recidivism" originated from the Latin word "recidīvus," which
      means "falling back." It came into English usage in the early 20th century.
    historical_usage: The term has been used primarily in criminology to describe
      the tendency of individuals to relapse into criminal behavior after being convicted.
  idioms:
  - expression: '"Cycle of recidivism"'
    explanation: This phrase highlights the repetitive pattern of criminal behavior
      that some individuals seem to experience, resulting in multiple convictions
      and prison terms.
  regional_variations: []
  misconceptions:
  - misconception: Confusing recidivism with relapse in the context of addiction.
    explanation: While the term "recidivism" is commonly associated with criminal
      behavior, it should not be confused with the term "relapse," which is primarily
      used to describe a return to addictive behavior after a period of abstinence.
      Recidivism specifically refers to repeated criminal activity.
