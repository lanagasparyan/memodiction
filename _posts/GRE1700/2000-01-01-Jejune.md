data:
  word: Jejune
  definitions:
  - part_of_speech: Adjective
    definitions:
    - Naive, simplistic, and superficial; lacking depth or substance.
    synonyms:
    - Naive
    - Simplistic
    - Shallow
    - Superficial
    antonyms:
    - Profound
    - Deep
    - Insightful
    examples:
    - His jejune arguments failed to convince the audience.
    - The book received negative reviews for its jejune plot.
    mnemonics:
    - Imagine a shallow and naive person trying to have a deep conversation and sounding
      jejune.
  etymology:
    origin: The word "jejune" originates from the Latin word "jejunus," meaning "fasting"
      or "hungry."
    historical_usage: Originally, "jejune" was used to describe something lacking
      in nutrition, such as food. Over time, it took on a figurative meaning of something
      lacking in substance or depth.
  idioms:
  - expression: '"Jejune literary style"'
    explanation: Refers to a writing style that is shallow, simplistic, and lacking
      in complexity or depth.
  regional_variations: []
  misconceptions:
  - misconception: Confusing "jejune" with "juvenile."
    explanation: While both words start with the same three letters, "jejune" pertains
      to the lack of depth or substance, whereas "juvenile" refers to something related
      to young people or immaturity.
