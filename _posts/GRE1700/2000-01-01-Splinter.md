data:
  word: Splinter
  definitions:
  - part_of_speech: Noun
    definitions:
    - A small, thin, sharp fragment, usually of wood or glass, that has separated
      from a larger object.
    synonyms:
    - Shard
    - Fragment
    - Sliver
    antonyms:
    - Whole
    - Intact
    - Unbroken
    examples:
    - He got a splinter in his finger from the wooden fence.
    - The window shattered, sending splinters flying everywhere.
    mnemonics:
    - Imagine a wooden splinter piercing through your skin.
  - part_of_speech: Verb
    definitions:
    - To break or cause to break into small, sharp fragments.
    synonyms:
    - Fragment
    - Shatter
    - Break
    antonyms:
    - Combine
    - Unite
    - Mend
    examples:
    - The impact of the hammer splintered the delicate glass vase.
    - The wind splintered the old tree, causing branches to fall in all directions.
    mnemonics:
    - Think of wood splitting into splinters under force.
  etymology:
    origin: Middle English 'splentour' from Old English 'spleinter', ultimately from
      Proto-Germanic 'splintar'.
    historical_usage: The word has been used since Middle English and has maintained
      its core meaning of a small sharp fragment.
  idioms:
  - expression: '"Splinter group"'
    explanation: A small group or faction that breaks away from a larger organization
      or movement.
  regional_variations: []
  misconceptions:
  - misconception: Confusing 'splinter' (noun) with 'splinter' (verb).
    explanation: '''Splinter'' can be both a noun and a verb with distinct meanings
      in each usage.'
