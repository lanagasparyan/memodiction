---
categories: [GRE, GRE_SET_2, TOEFL, English]
data:
  word: Implicate
  definitions:
  - part_of_speech: Verb
    definitions:
    - To show or suggest that someone is involved in a crime or wrongdoing.
    - To connect or involve someone or something in a complicated or perplexing situation.
    synonyms:
    - Involve
    - Incriminate
    - Embroil
    antonyms:
    - Absolve
    - Exonerate
    - Clear
    examples:
    - The evidence seems to implicate him in the robbery.
    - His careless actions implicated his entire team in the scandal.
    mnemonics:
    - '"Imply" someone is innocent, but "implicate" someone in a crime.'
    - '"Complicate" often goes hand in hand with implicating someone.'
  etymology:
    origin: The word "implicate" comes from the Latin word "implicare", which means
      'to involve' or 'to entwine.'
    historical_usage: The word has been used since the 16th century in the sense of
      involving someone in a specific context, such as crimes or complicated situations.
  idioms:
  - expression: '"Implicate oneself"'
    explanation: This phrase refers to someone voluntarily involving themselves in
      a situation or admitting their own guilt.
  regional_variations: []
  misconceptions:
  - misconception: Confusing "implicate" with "imply."
    explanation: While "implicate" suggests involvement or connection, "imply" means
      to express something indirectly or hint at it. These terms have distinct meanings
      and should not be used interchangeably.
