  word: Didactic
  definitions:
  - part_of_speech: Adjective
    definitions:
    - Intended to teach or instruct; inclined to teach or lecture others excessively.
    synonyms:
    - Instructive
    - Educational
    - Informative
    antonyms:
    - Uninformative
    - Non-educational
    examples:
    - The professor's lecture was highly didactic, providing valuable insights into
      the subject matter.
    - The novel had a didactic tone, with the author attempting to convey moral lessons
      to the readers.
    mnemonics:
    - '"Didactic" sounds like "didacticity," which relates to the quality of being
      instructive.'
  etymology:
    origin: The word "didactic" comes from the Greek word "didaktikos," meaning "apt
      at teaching."
    historical_usage: The term "didactic" has been used since the late 17th century
      to describe works or individuals that have a teaching or instructive purpose.
  idioms:
  - expression: '"Didactic literature"'
    explanation: Refers to works of literature that are intended to educate or convey
      moral, religious, or philosophical lessons.
  regional_variations: []
  misconceptions:
  - misconception: Using "didactic" as a negative term to describe something that
      is overly preachy or moralizing.
    explanation: While "didactic" can sometimes carry a negative connotation when
      used to criticize works that are excessively instructive or lacking subtlety,
      it primarily refers to the intention or purpose of teaching and instructing
      rather than being inherently negative.
