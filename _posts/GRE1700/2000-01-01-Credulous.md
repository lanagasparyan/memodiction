---
categories: [GRE, GRE_SET_2, TOEFL, English]
data:
  word: Credulous
  definitions:
  - part_of_speech: Adjective
    definitions:
    - Too ready to believe things; gullible.
    synonyms:
    - Naive
    - Trusting
    - Unsuspecting
    antonyms:
    - Skeptical
    - Incredulous
    - Wary
    examples:
    - The credulous child believed the magician could actually make things disappear.
    - The scammer took advantage of her credulous nature and convinced her to give
      him her life savings.
    mnemonics:
    - Credulous sounds similar to "credible," but it means the opposite of being reliable
      or trustworthy. Think of a person blindly giving too much credit to information
      or claims.
  etymology:
    origin: Late Middle English from Latin 'credulus', from 'credere' meaning "to
      believe."
    historical_usage: The word "credulous" has been in use since the late 15th century,
      maintaining its core definition of being willing to believe things without critical
      evaluation.
  idioms:
  - expression: '"Credulous as a child"'
    explanation: Referring to someone who is highly trusting or easily persuaded,
      much like how children tend to be less skeptical and more accepting of information.
  regional_variations: []
  misconceptions:
  - misconception: Confusing "credulous" with "incredulous."
    explanation: '"Credulous" means being too ready to believe, while "incredulous"
      means being unwilling or unable to believe. The prefix "in-" in "incredulous"
      negates the word, making it the opposite of "credulous."'
