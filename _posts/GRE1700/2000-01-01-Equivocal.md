---
categories: [GRE, GRE_SET_2, TOEFL, English]
data:
  word: Equivocal
  definitions:
  - part_of_speech: Adjective
    definitions:
    - Open to more than one interpretation; ambiguous.
    synonyms:
    - Ambiguous
    - Unclear
    - Vague
    - Cryptic
    antonyms:
    - Clear
    - Definite
    - Straightforward
    examples:
    - His equivocal response left everyone confused.
    - The equivocal statement could be interpreted in different ways.
    mnemonics:
    - Think of "equi" meaning "equal" or "same" and "vocal" meaning "voice" or "expression."
      Equivocal refers to multiple equal or ambiguous expressions.
  etymology:
    origin: Late Middle English from Latin 'aequivocus' (from 'aequi-' meaning "equal"
      and 'vocare' meaning "to call") + '-al'.
    historical_usage: Equivocal has been used in English since the late 15th century
      with its current meaning of ambiguous or open to interpretation.
  idioms:
  - expression: '"Speak with an equivocal voice"'
    explanation: To give ambiguous or unclear statements, often to avoid commitment
      or taking a clear stance.
  regional_variations: []
  misconceptions:
  - misconception: Equivocal means the same as univocal or unequivocal.
    explanation: Equivocal means ambiguous or open to multiple interpretations, while
      univocal or unequivocal means clear and definite. These words have opposite
      meanings.
