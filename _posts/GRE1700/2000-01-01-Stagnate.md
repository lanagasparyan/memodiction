categories: [GRE, GRE_SET_2, TOEFL, English]
data:
  word: Stagnate
  definitions:
  - part_of_speech: Verb
    definitions:
    - Cease developing or advancing; become inactive or dull.
    synonyms:
    - Idle
    - Languish
    - Stall
    - Stand still
    antonyms:
    - Progress
    - Thrive
    examples:
    - With no new ideas, the company's growth stagnated.
    - Without proper maintenance, the water in the pond will stagnate.
    mnemonics:
    - '"Stagnate" sounds like "stagnant," which refers to something that is not moving
      or progressing.'
  etymology:
    origin: Late Latin "stagnatus," from the verb "stagnare" meaning "to stagnate
      or stand still."
    historical_usage: The word has been used in English since the late 17th century
      to describe the lack of movement or development.
  idioms:
  - expression: '"Stagnate like a pond"'
    explanation: This expression highlights the idea that when something stagnates,
      it becomes inactive, dull, or stagnant just like water in a pond that lacks
      movement.
  regional_variations: []
  misconceptions:
  - misconception: Confusing "stagnate" with other words like "stale" or "decay."
    explanation: While all these words refer to lack of development or change, they
      have different nuances and should not be used interchangeably. "Stagnate" specifically
      refers to a cessation of development or becoming dull, while "stale" refers
      to something no longer fresh or interesting, and "decay" refers to the gradual
      deterioration or decomposition of something.
