data:
  word: Truce
  definitions:
  - part_of_speech: Noun
    definitions:
    - An agreement between enemies or opponents to stop fighting or arguing for a
      certain time.
    synonyms:
    - Ceasefire
    - Armistice
    - Peace
    antonyms:
    - Conflict
    - War
    - Disagreement
    examples:
    - The two countries signed a truce, ending the long-standing conflict.
    - The siblings called a truce and decided to stop arguing for the rest of the
      day.
    mnemonics:
    - Think of "true" in "truce" indicating a momentary pause in hostility or disagreement.
    - Picture shaking hands symbolizing an agreement to temporarily stop fighting
      or arguing.
  etymology:
    origin: Middle English, from Old English 'trēow', meaning "faith, covenant, pledge",
      and influenced by Old Norse 'traust' meaning "faith, confidence".
    historical_usage: The word "truce" has been used in various contexts throughout
      history, primarily to describe temporary suspensions of hostilities during times
      of conflict between nations or factions.
  idioms:
  - expression: '"Break a truce"'
    explanation: To violate or end a temporary agreement to cease hostilities or arguments.
  regional_variations: []
  misconceptions:
  - misconception: Using "truce" interchangeably with "peace".
    explanation: While both "truce" and "peace" imply a cessation of conflict, a truce
      is a temporary agreement with the understanding that hostilities may resume,
      whereas peace implies a more permanent state of harmony or absence of war.
