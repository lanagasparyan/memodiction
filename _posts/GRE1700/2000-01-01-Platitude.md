---
categories: [GRE, GRE_SET_2, TOEFL, English]
data:
  word: Platitude
  definitions:
  - part_of_speech: Noun
    definitions:
    - A remark or statement, especially one with a moral content, that has been used
      too often to be interesting or thoughtful.
    synonyms:
    - Cliché
    - Truism
    - Banality
    - Commonplace
    antonyms:
    - Originality
    - Innovation
    examples:
    - His speech was filled with empty platitudes about success and happiness.
    - She grew tired of hearing the same platitudes about love and relationships.
    mnemonics:
    - '"Platitude" sounds like "plat" (flat) attitude, suggesting a lack of depth
      or originality.'
  etymology:
    origin: The word "platitude" originated from the French word "plat" meaning "flat"
      or "dull". It entered the English language in the mid 18th century.
    historical_usage: The term gained popularity in the 19th century as a way to describe
      overused and insincere expressions.
  idioms:
  - expression: '"Spouting platitudes"'
    explanation: Referring to someone who mindlessly repeats common, unoriginal statements.
  regional_variations: []
  misconceptions:
  - misconception: Considering all moral or repeated statements as platitudes.
    explanation: Not all statements that have a moral content or are frequently used
      are platitudes. Only those that lack originality or sincerity can be considered
      as such.
