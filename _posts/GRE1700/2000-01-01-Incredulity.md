data:
  word: Incredulity
  definitions:
  - part_of_speech: Noun
    definitions:
    - The state of being unwilling or unable to believe something.
    synonyms:
    - Disbelief
    - Skepticism
    - Doubt
    antonyms:
    - Belief
    - Trust
    - Acceptance
    examples:
    - His incredulity regarding the existence of aliens was evident in his skeptical

    - The detective's incredulity towards the suspect's alibi raised some doubts about
      his innocence.
    mnemonics:
    - '"Incredulity" sounds like "incredible" which means something so unbelievable
      that it generates doubt or disbelief.'
  etymology:
    origin: The word "incredulity" originated from the Latin word "incredulus," which
      means "not believing" or "unbelieving."
    historical_usage: The term "incredulity" has been used in English since at least
      the 14th century, to express the state of being unwilling or unable to believe
      something.
  idioms:
  - expression: '"Raise an eyebrow in incredulity"'
    explanation: This phrase implies showing surprise, doubt, or disbelief by raising
      one's eyebrow. It signifies the disbelief or skepticism towards something unexpected
      or unusual.
  regional_variations: []
  misconceptions:
  - misconception: Confusing "incredulity" with "credulity."
    explanation: '"Credulity" refers to a tendency to believe something too readily,
      while "incredulity" refers to a state of skepticism or doubt. These words have
      opposite meanings and should not be used interchangeably.'
