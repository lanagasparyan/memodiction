  etymology:
    origin: The word "infuriate" originated from the Latin word "in" (into) + "furiare"
      (to enrage) which comes from "furor" (fury).
    historical_usage: The word has been used in English since the late 16th century
      to describe extreme anger or rage.
  idioms:
  - expression: '"Infuriated to no end"'
    explanation: This phrase means being extremely angry without any limit or bounds.
  regional_variations: []
  misconceptions:
  - misconception: Using "infuriate" synonymously with "annoy" or "bother."
