data:
  word: Euthanasia
  definitions:
  - part_of_speech: Noun
    definitions:
    - The act or practice of intentionally ending the life of a person or animal in
      order to relieve their suffering, usually done by a physician or veterinarian.
    synonyms:
    - Mercy killing
    - Assisted suicide
    antonyms:
    - Cure
    - Treatment
    examples:
    - The patient chose euthanasia as a way to end their pain.
    - The veterinarian performed euthanasia on the old and sick dog to prevent further
      suffering.
    mnemonics:
    - Think of 'euthanasia' as 'eu' (meaning good) + 'thanatos' (Greek for death),
      implying a good death or release from suffering.
  etymology:
    origin: The term 'euthanasia' is derived from the Greek words 'eu' (meaning good)
      and 'thanatos' (meaning death).
    historical_usage: The concept of euthanasia dates back to ancient Greece, where
      it was often considered an acceptable form of death in certain situations. The
      modern ethical debate surrounding euthanasia began in the 19th century and continues
      to be a topic of discussion and controversy.
  idioms:
  - expression: '"Voluntary euthanasia"'
    explanation: Refers to euthanasia performed with the explicit consent of the person
      or their legal representative.
  regional_variations: []
  misconceptions:
  - misconception: Equating euthanasia with murder or suicide.
    explanation: Euthanasia is performed with the intention of relieving suffering,
      guided by ethical considerations and certain legal frameworks. It is distinct
      from murder or suicide, which involve malicious intent or personal choice without
      medical or compassionate reasons.
