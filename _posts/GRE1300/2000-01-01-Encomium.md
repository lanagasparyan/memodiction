  - part_of_speech: Noun

    - Accolade
    - Commendation
    - Panegyric
    antonyms:
    - Criticism
    - Censure
    - Condemnation
    examples:

    - '"Encomium" sounds like "en-compliment," reminding you of its meaning as praise.'
  etymology:
    origin: Latin "encomium" or Greek "enkōmion", originally derived from the verb
      "enkōmein" meaning "to accompany" or "to celebrate.”
    historical_usage: The word has been used since ancient times, often in the context
      of praising someone or something, such as in speeches or written tributes.
  idioms:
  - expression: '"Sing someone''s encomium"'
    explanation: To deliver a heartfelt and glowing tribute or praise about someone.
  regional_variations: []
  misconceptions:
  - misconception: Confusing "encomium" with "incongruum."
    explanation: Despite the similar sound, "incongruum" means something that is incongruous
      or out of place, while "encomium" refers to warm praise or tribute.
