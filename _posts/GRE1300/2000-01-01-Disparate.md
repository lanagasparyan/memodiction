---
categories: [GRE, GRE_SET_1, TOEFL, English]
data:
  word: Disparate
  definitions:
  - part_of_speech: Adjective
    definitions:
    - Essentially different; unrelated; distinct.
    synonyms:
    - Different
    - Diverse
    - Distinct
    antonyms:
    - Similar
    - Alike
    - Uniform
    examples:
    - The two paintings displayed in the gallery are disparate in style and subject
      matter.
    - The committee members held disparate opinions on how to approach the issue.
    mnemonics:
    - Think of "dis-" as a negation prefix meaning "not" and "parate" as "pair" or
      "related." Disparate means not related or essentially different.
  etymology:
    origin: Late Middle English from Latin 'disparatus', past participle of 'disparare',
      from 'dis-' (apart, differently) + 'parare' (make ready or prepare).
    historical_usage: The term has been used since the late 15th century, primarily
      in English-speaking contexts.
  idioms:
  - expression: '"Disparate impact"'
    explanation: Refers to a legal term used in discrimination law to describe a situation
      where a particular policy or practice, although seemingly neutral, disproportionately
      affects a certain group of people, leading to discrimination.
  regional_variations: []
  misconceptions:
  - misconception: Confusing "disparate" with "desperate."
    explanation: These two words have different meanings and should not be used interchangeably.
      "Disparate" means essentially different, while "desperate" refers to a state
      of extreme need or urgency.
