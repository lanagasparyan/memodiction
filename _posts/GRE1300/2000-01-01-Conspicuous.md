  - part_of_speech: Adjective

    - Noticeable

    - Evident

    - Unnoticeable

    - '"Conspicuous" sounds similar to "obvious."'

    origin: Latin "conspicuus," from "conspicere" meaning "to observe"
    historical_usage: The word "conspicuous" has been used in English since the mid-16th
      century, derived from its Latin origin. It has retained its meaning of being
      easily seen or remarkable over the centuries.
  idioms:
  - expression: '"Conspicuous by one''s absence"'
    explanation: Refers to someone's absence being so noticeable that it becomes a
      topic of discussion. It implies that the person's presence was expected or necessary
      in a particular situation.
  regional_variations: []
  misconceptions:
  - misconception: Confusing "conspicuous" with "suspicious."
