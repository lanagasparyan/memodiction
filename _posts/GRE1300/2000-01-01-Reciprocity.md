---
categories: [GRE, GRE_SET_1, TOEFL, English]
data:
  word: Reciprocity
  definitions:
  - part_of_speech: Noun
    definitions:
    - The practice of exchanging things with others for mutual benefit, especially
      privileges or favors.
    synonyms:
    - Mutual exchange
    - Give-and-take
    - Interchange
    antonyms:
    - One-sidedness
    - Imbalance
    examples:
    - The two countries have a reciprocal agreement that allows citizens to travel
      freely between them.
    - In a healthy relationship, there is a sense of reciprocity where both partners
      support and care for each other.
    mnemonics:
    - Think of a seesaw - reciprocity is like a balanced exchange of weight on both
      sides.
  etymology:
    origin: Latin "reciprocus" meaning "alternating"
    historical_usage: The concept of reciprocity dates back to ancient times, with
      references to reciprocal relationships found in various cultures and societies.
  idioms:
  - expression: '"Reciprocity principle"'
    explanation: Refers to the ethical principle that individuals should treat others
      as they wish to be treated themselves, or that favors, benefits, or privileges
      should be returned in kind.
  regional_variations: []
  misconceptions:
  - misconception: Confusing reciprocity with "tit for tat" or retaliation.
    explanation: Reciprocity is based on a fair exchange or mutual benefit, whereas
      "tit for tat" involves responding to an action with an equivalent action, often
      out of retaliation. Reciprocity is a more positive and balanced concept.
