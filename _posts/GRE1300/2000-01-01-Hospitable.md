    - Welcoming
    - Friendly
    - Accommodating

    - Inhospitable

  etymology:
    origin: The word "hospitable" originated from the Latin word "hospes," which means
      "guest" or "host." It can be divided into two parts - "hospes" and "-abilis"
      (a suffix meaning "able to").
    historical_usage: The term has been used in English since the 15th century, derived
      from the Latin concept of hospes as both a guest and a host, emphasizing the
      reciprocity of hospitality.
  idioms:
  - expression: '"Rolling out the red carpet for someone"'
    explanation: This phrase is often used to describe a very hospitable and grand
      welcome provided to someone, as if they were a VIP or celebrity.
  regional_variations: []
  misconceptions:
  - misconception: Confusing "hospitable" with "hospitality."
    explanation: '"Hospitable" is an adjective describing the characteristic of being
      welcoming and generous, while "hospitality" is a noun that refers to the act
      or practice of being hospitable.'
