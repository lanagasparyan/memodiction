data:
  word: Eddy
  definitions:
  - part_of_speech: Noun
    definitions:
    - A circular or spiral movement of air or water, especially one that creates a
      small whirlpool.
    synonyms:
    - Whirlpool
    - Vortex
    - Swirl
    antonyms:
    - Calm
    - Stillness
    examples:
    - The strong wind created an eddy in the river.
    - I watched the leaves swirling in the eddy of the stream.
    mnemonics:
    - Picture a whirlpool or vortex when thinking of eddy.
    - The word eddy rhymes with ready, and you can think of an eddy as a swirling
      motion getting ready to suck things in.
  etymology:
    origin: Middle English 'edyen', from Old English 'ēðian', related to Old High
      German 'walzen', meaning "to roll, to wander".
    historical_usage: The term eddy has been used since Middle English to describe
      a swirling movement of air or water.
  idioms:
  - expression: '"Caught in an eddy"'
    explanation: Refers to being trapped or caught in a swirling or circular current,
      often used figuratively to describe being stuck in a repetitive or unproductive
      cycle.
  regional_variations: []
  misconceptions:
  - misconception: Considering an eddy as a synonym for a whirlwind or tornado.
    explanation: While eddies and whirlwinds may share similarities in their swirling
      motions, an eddy typically occurs in a contained area such as a river or stream,
      while a whirlwind or tornado is a much larger and more powerful atmospheric
      phenomenon.
