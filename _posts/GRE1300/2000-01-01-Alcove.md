    - Open space

    origin: Late Middle English from Old French 'alcove', from Spanish 'alcoba' or
      Arabic 'al-qubba' meaning "vaulted arch, dome".
    historical_usage: The term "alcove" originated from Arabic and was introduced
      into the English language during the Middle Ages.
  idioms:
  - expression: '"Alcove bed"'
    explanation: Refers to a bed built into an alcove, usually with walls on three
      sides, providing a cozy and enclosed sleeping space.
  regional_variations: []
  misconceptions:
  - misconception: Using "alcove" interchangeably with "nook" or "corner" without
      considering the partially enclosed aspect.

      enclosed space, while a nook or corner can be open or fully enclosed.
